01 / The build

What went on it.

My first build

Four motors on three-inch props, one 4S battery, a flight controller that holds the attitude, and a video transmitter that feeds the goggles.

None of the parts came with a model. The airframe is the only thing here that exists as CAD; the rest was measured by hand and drawn around, so nothing could be checked before it was made.

04 / What I'd do differently

Design the system.

The order that matters

This was my first build, and I did it backwards: the frame first, the parts after. Every dimension came off a component with a ruler and went straight into the airframe, with no way to check the fit until it was physical. When something did not line up, the drawing was already wrong.

If I built it again, the frame would be the last thing I designed — not the first. The parts would be models before they were purchases, and the assembly would be checked on screen before anything was made.

  1. 01

    Model every part first. Boards, mounts, the camera cage, the battery envelope — in CAD before the frame is drawn.

  2. 02

    Fix the mounting geometry. Which part bolts to which, at what holes, at what height.

  3. 03

    Know the mass and the centre of gravity. So the frame does not decide them for you.

  4. 04

    Bench-test the stack. Video link, control link, motor order and attitude — on the ground first.

  5. 05

    Design the frame last. Around the assembly, not around guesses.

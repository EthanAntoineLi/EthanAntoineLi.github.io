01 / The assistant

What she actually does.

Built around my needs

  1. 01

    Long-term memory

    Context carries across sessions. A project or a reminder can be picked up later instead of explained again.

  2. 02

    Reminders

    A sentence becomes a timed nudge. She comes back when it’s due.

  3. 03

    Morning timetable

    The day’s timetable arrives every morning, before I ask for it.

  4. 04

    Practice questions

    Topic-specific questions pulled from my question bank. A dedicated question-maker sub-agent drafts them; the main agent wakes it when needed.

02 / The build

A framework, and
everything around it.

Layered, not monolithic

OpenClaw is the base framework — the part that already existed. Everything that makes Nora useful is built around it, one layer per need: memory, reminders, the timetable, the question bank.

Work that needs its own focus gets a specialist sub-agent; the main agent decides when to wake it. Nora runs on a dedicated Mac, 24/7 — the whole machine is hers, not an app I open.

03 / The interface

An agent needs
a face.

160 × 90 px · 15 fps

An always-on assistant is invisible when it’s working. The cat makes the state readable from across the room: what she’s doing, and whether she’s even there.

  • Offline asleep in a cardboard box; the room dims
  • Idle blinks, grooms, follows the cursor; naps after five minutes
  • Listening ears up; the message appears in a bubble
  • Thinking looks up, thought bubble
  • Working types on a tiny laptop; the bubble names the tool
  • Replying talks; the answer appears in a bubble
  • Done hops with sparkles
  • Error puffs up, ears flat, hisses

She also idles like a cat: grooming, yawning, chasing a butterfly, sitting in her box. Click her to pet her; six quick pokes make her grumpy; poke her while she’s working and she tells you she’s busy. The room is drawn in code on a 160 × 90 canvas at 15 fps, and the sounds are synthesised 8-bit meows, purrs and hisses.

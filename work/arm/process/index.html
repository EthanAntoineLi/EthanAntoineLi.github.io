01 / How it all started

A 3D printer,
then parts of my own.

The 3D printer is what gave me access to personalised parts — pieces designed for this arm instead of picked out of a supplier’s catalogue. That is the part that changed everything: the design stopped being limited to what someone else already sells.

One printer · geometry to my design · the arm came after

02 / Motors

Five motors,
two sizes.

The joints run on five steppers — one UMIR57 at the base, four UMIR42s further out — all on one RS485 / Modbus-RTU bus. Picking them was the first time the build had to match a datasheet.

UMIR57 ×1 · UMIR42 ×4 · RS485 / Modbus-RTU

03 / Printing

Printed here,
layer by layer.

The arm’s printed parts came off this A1 mini, one layer at a time — on screen, the link between joint 1 and joint 2. The models are free to download: the whole arm, the printer, and every part on its own.

Printing link J1 → J2 0%

04 / Revision

One link,
drawn twice.

One part changed on the way: the link between joint 1 and joint 2, from the base to the shoulder. The first design is on the left, the one on the arm now on the right.

Link J1 → J2 · base → shoulder · rev A → rev B

05 / Design

Six axes,
drawn in CAD.

Every part of the arm was modelled before it was made: six axes on screen, five of them built. The printed parts were sized to the motors instead of picked to fit a catalogue — that is what the printer made possible.

6 axes drawn · 5 built · CAD assembly

06 / Assemble

Five joints,
one arm.

Printed parts, motors and wiring go together into one five-axis arm, each joint fitted around the parts it has to hold. What came out of this bench is the machine on sheet 01.

printed parts · five motors · one arm

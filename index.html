01 Mission

Someone is hurt on a slope that’s hard to reach. The rescue team has to walk into the same ground to get them out.

I ski. Every winter I see the same shape of accident: a person down on a line that is difficult, exposed, or both — and a team that has to commit people to that ground to reach them. The question at the top of this page isn’t rhetorical. It’s the reason I started building.

The system I’m working towards is two machines and a handoff. The drone finds the person and stays overhead. The arm does the reaching and the lifting. Each half is a real prototype; the handoff between them does not exist yet. That distinction matters to me more than a demo that looks finished.

02 The arm

The first piece: a five-axis arm I designed, printed, and articulated.

This is my EPQ artefact. The constraint that shaped every part was buildability: it has to be printable on one Bambu Lab H2S and assemble from hardware I can actually buy.

Six degrees of freedom were designed. Five were built — the budget stopped at five motors. All five joints are driven by steppers through planetary and worm reductions, commanded over RS485/Modbus from an STM32F407. The base carries a 25:1 planetary; the shoulder uses a self-locking 50:1 worm, so it holds position without drawing current.

03 The drone

The second piece is a camera that can fly. The software that makes it useful does not exist yet.

I fly it as an FPV drone: a pilot, goggles, a video feed. In the rescue system it is meant to be the first thing on scene — find the injured person, hold position, and hand a location to the arm. Right now only the flying is real.

The recognition and spatial-positioning layer is the next build, not a feature I can claim. When it works there will be a second video on this page; until then this section stays deliberately short.
